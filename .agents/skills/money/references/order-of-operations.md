# Order of operations

The Financial Order of Operations is a waterfall for the next dollar. Finish or skip with a reason before you fund a later step.

Generic thresholds. Not a household's private targets. Adjust only when the human states a fact.

## Steps

1. Starter cash. Cover the highest insurance deductible, or $1,000 if that number is unknown. This keeps a small shock off a card.
2. Employer match. Contribute the minimum that captures the full match in a workplace plan. Skip if there is no employer plan.
3. High-interest debt. Avalanche. Pay minimums on every balance. Put every extra dollar on the highest APR. A common high-interest cutoff is 7 to 8 percent. Revolving cards count here even during a 0 percent teaser. State the cutoff.
4. Emergency reserve. Three to six months of essential expenses. Essentials are housing, food, utilities, insurance, transport, and required minimums. Six months if income is variable or there is one earner.
5. Tax-advantaged accounts. Fund a Roth or traditional IRA up to the annual limit you are eligible for. Fund an HSA if a high-deductible plan makes you eligible.
6. Max employer plans. Increase 401(k), 403(b), or similar contributions toward the annual elective-deferral limit.
7. Hyper-accumulation. Aim for about 25 percent of gross income toward retirement across every vehicle already opened.
8. Prepay future expenses. Date-certain costs. Education, a down payment, a relocation. Use a separate named bucket.
9. Prepay low-interest debt. Mortgage or cheap installment loans. Only after steps 1 through 8 are honest.

## Avalanche

List debts by APR descending. Ignore balance size when picking the target. Snowball is allowed only if the human asks for motivation over math. Say which method you used.

## Glass box

Every claim in `plan.md` must point at a ledger line, a bill id, or a sentence the human wrote. If the files do not show an APR, do not invent one. If cash on hand is below starter cash, the plan stays on step 1.
